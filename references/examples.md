# Before/After Examples

Each example shows a specific pattern being fixed. Use these as calibration for your rewrites.

---

## Significance inflation

**Before:**
> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was officially established in 1989, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of regional statistics in Spain. This initiative was part of a broader movement across Spain to decentralize administrative functions and enhance regional governance.

**After:**
> The Statistical Institute of Catalonia was established in 1989 to collect and publish regional statistics independently from Spain's national statistics office.

**What changed:** Removed "marking a pivotal moment," "evolution of," "broader movement," "enhance regional governance." Replaced with the specific purpose.

---

## Promotional language

**Before:**
> Nestled within the breathtaking region of Gonder in Ethiopia, Alamata Raya Kobo stands as a vibrant town with a rich cultural heritage and stunning natural beauty.

**After:**
> Alamata Raya Kobo is a town in the Gonder region of Ethiopia, known for its weekly market and 18th-century church.

**What changed:** Removed "nestled," "breathtaking," "stands as," "vibrant," "rich cultural heritage," "stunning natural beauty." Replaced with two specific facts.

---

## Superficial -ing phrases

**Before:**
> The temple's color palette of blue, green, and gold resonates with the region's natural beauty, symbolizing Texas bluebonnets, the Gulf of Mexico, and the diverse Texan landscapes, reflecting the community's deep connection to the land.

**After:**
> The temple uses blue, green, and gold colors. The architect said these were chosen to reference local bluebonnets and the Gulf coast.

**What changed:** Removed "symbolizing," "reflecting," "resonates with," "deep connection." Split into two direct sentences with a specific attribution.

---

## Em dash removal

**Before:**
> The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions—not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe" as an address—yet this mislabeling continues—even in official documents.

**After:**
> The term is primarily promoted by Dutch institutions, not by the people themselves. You don't say "Netherlands, Europe" as an address, yet this mislabeling continues in official documents.

**What changed:** Three em dashes replaced with commas or restructured.

---

## Rule of three

**Before:**
> The event features keynote sessions, panel discussions, and networking opportunities. Attendees can expect innovation, inspiration, and industry insights.

**After:**
> The event includes talks and panels. There's also time for informal networking between sessions.

**What changed:** Two rule-of-three patterns broken. Second sentence's triple eliminated entirely.

---

## Binary contrast

**Before:**
> Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in.

**After:**
> Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't.

**What changed:** Removed throat-clearing opener, binary contrast structure, and emphasis crutch. Direct statements.

---

## Copula avoidance

**Before:**
> Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's exhibition space for contemporary art. The gallery features four separate spaces and boasts over 3,000 square feet.

**After:**
> Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space for contemporary art. The gallery has four rooms totaling 3,000 square feet.

**What changed:** "serves as" -> "is". "features" -> [restructured]. "boasts" -> "has".

---

## Filler + hedging

**Before:**
> In order to achieve optimal results, it is important to note that the system has the ability to process data efficiently. Due to the fact that it was designed with scalability in mind, it could potentially handle large volumes.

**After:**
> The system processes data fast and handles large volumes.

**What changed:** "In order to" -> cut. "it is important to note that" -> cut. "has the ability to" -> "can" (then simplified). "Due to the fact that" -> cut. "could potentially" -> reduced to direct claim.

---

## Performative candor

**Before:**
> The honest answer is, most startups fail because the founders run out of energy. Let's cut through the noise. It's not funding, it's not product-market fit. Here's the thing: building a company is exhausting, and nobody talks about that enough.

**After:**
> Most startups fail because the founders run out of energy. Not funding, not product-market fit. Building a company is exhausting and that doesn't get talked about enough.

**What changed:** Removed "The honest answer is," "Let's cut through the noise," "Here's the thing." The content is the same — it just stopped announcing its own honesty.

---

## Anaphora

**Before:**
> Your sales team gets an estimating tool. Your ops manager gets a lookup tool. Your front desk gets an intake assistant.

**After:**
> The estimating tool pulls from your actual pricing for the sales team. Ops gets a lookup against old project files. The front desk intake assistant pre-fills onboarding from a single form.

**What changed:** Three consecutive "Your [role] gets..." broken by varying the sentence subjects and structures.

---

## False agency

**Before:**
> The data tells us that the market rewards innovation. The conversation has moved toward sustainability. The culture is shifting.

**After:**
> We found that buyers choose newer products more often. More companies now talk about sustainability in earnings calls. Three of our clients changed their procurement policies this year.

**What changed:** Inanimate subjects replaced with human actors and specific claims.

## Full transformation (combining all patterns)

**Before:**
> Great question! Here is an essay on this topic. In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, AI-assisted coding serves as an enduring testament to the transformative potential of large language models, marking a pivotal moment in the evolution of software development. These groundbreaking tools — nestled at the intersection of research and practice — are reshaping how engineers ideate, iterate, and deliver, underscoring their vital role in modern workflows. It's not just about autocomplete; it's about unlocking creativity at scale. The tool serves as a catalyst. The assistant functions as a partner. The system stands as a foundation.

**After:**
> AI coding assistants can make you faster at the boring parts. Not everything. They're great at boilerplate: config files, test scaffolding, repetitive refactors. They're also great at sounding right while being wrong. I've accepted suggestions that compiled and passed lint but still missed the point because I stopped paying attention.

**What changed:** Removed chatbot artifacts, significance inflation ("testament," "pivotal moment," "evolving landscape"), promotional language ("groundbreaking," "nestled"), em dashes, binary contrast ("not just X, it's Y"), copula avoidance ("serves as," "functions as," "stands as"), rule-of-three, superficial vocabulary ("transformative," "vital," "underscore"). Added first-person voice, specific experience, and human rhythm.
