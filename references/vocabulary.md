## Tier 2: REPLACE UNLESS LITERAL/TECHNICAL

These words are fine when used in their literal meaning. Replace when used figuratively.

| Word | OK When | Replace When |
|---|---|---|
| align | physical alignment, text alignment | "align with our goals" -> "match our goals" |
| architect (verb) | actual architecture | "architect a solution" -> "design a solution" |
| craft (verb) | actual craftsmanship | "craft a message" -> "write a message" |
| cultivate | actual agriculture | "cultivate relationships" -> "build relationships" |
| curate | actual museum/gallery work | "curated list" -> "selected list" |
| deep dive | actual diving | "deep dive into" -> "close look at" |
| ecosystem | actual biology | "startup ecosystem" -> "startup community" |
| framework | actual structures, software | "leadership framework" -> "approach" |
| granular | actual materials science | "granular detail" -> "specific detail" |
| leverage | mechanical advantage | "leverage our brand" -> "use our brand" |
| landscape | actual geography | "competitive landscape" -> "competitive situation" |
| navigate | actual movement/sailing | "navigate challenges" -> "handle challenges" |
| organic | chemistry, farming | "organic growth" -> "natural growth" |
| pipeline | plumbing, software | "talent pipeline" -> "hiring plan" |
| platform | physical platforms | "leverage our platform" -> "use our product" |

| unpack | actual luggage | "unpack this idea" -> "explain this" |

## Tier 3: CONTEXT-DEPENDENT DENSITY FLAGS

These words are fine alone but become AI tells when 2+ appear in the same paragraph:

significant, innovative, effective, strategic, dynamic, diverse, sustainable, inclusive, comprehensive, meaningful, impactful, transformative, collaborative, proactive, scalable

**Rule:** If you find 2+ Tier 3 words in the same paragraph, replace all but one with specific descriptions of what you mean.

## Allowed -ly words (whitelist)

Version 1.0 banned every -ly adverb. That made rewrites stiff and sometimes wrong. These words are allowed when they carry real meaning, appear sparingly (about one per paragraph at most), and could not be cut without changing what the sentence says.

| Word | Allowed when | Still a tell when |
|---|---|---|
| only | it limits something ("only two seats left") | used for emphasis ("I only want to say") |
| likely | it states a real probability | stacked with other hedges ("likely could potentially") |
| early | time ("early March", "the early draft") | never |
| nearly | a real near miss or amount ("nearly $4,000") | padding ("nearly always") |
| really | casual speech and texts, once | repeated or used to inflate ("really, really important") |
| simply | literal meaning, "in a simple way" ("simply put the key under the mat") | dismissive filler ("simply put,") |
| finally | the last step in a real sequence, or relief after a wait | a transition word between paragraphs |
| directly | a real path with no middle step ("buy directly from the farm") | emphasis ("speak directly to") |
| recently | a date the reader needs | vague time filler |
| usually | a real pattern | a hedge in front of a claim you could state |
| probably | honest uncertainty, once | stacked hedging |
| exactly | precise amounts or matches | "exactly what you need" |
| daily, weekly, monthly, yearly | schedules and frequencies | never |
| actually | contradicting something specific, in casual registers | performative candor ("what actually matters") |

Words where -ly is part of the root (family, apply, supply, reply, rally, holy, Italy, July, early, ugly, friendly as an adjective) were never adverbs and are always fine.

**Still banned:** intensifiers and filler adverbs. truly, deeply, incredibly, seamlessly, fundamentally, ultimately, essentially, significantly, remarkably, absolutely, undeniably, arguably, notably, importantly, interestingly, crucially. Replace with a stronger verb or cut.

**Density rule:** two or more whitelisted -ly words in one sentence, or more than one per paragraph in formal writing, counts as a violation. Cut down to the one that matters.
